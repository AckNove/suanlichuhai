# Customer Service Introduction: From Compute Requirement to a Decision-Ready Plan

## 1. Our role in your procurement

We are not a GPU reseller, and we are not a middle layer that marks up a quotation. We are a **buy-side compute procurement advisor**: we help you define the requirement, identify the options available globally, verify the suppliers, and put comparable plans and risks in front of you so that you decide who to buy from and how.

Wherever possible we aim for you to contract and settle directly with the actual capacity provider. Our value is in the process, not in a markup on the price.

## 2. Step one: turn "we need compute" into a purchasable specification

Most compute procurement problems start with an undefined requirement. We work through the following with you:

| Dimension | What we confirm |
|---|---|
| Workload | Training / fine-tuning / inference / rendering / simulation? Mixed workloads? |
| Model and scale | Model type, parameter count, parallelism strategy, memory requirement |
| GPU type | Preferred model and why; whether alternatives are acceptable |
| Quantity and duration | Number of GPUs, continuous duration, expected peaks |
| Cluster and interconnect | Cluster size, intra-/inter-node interconnect, RDMA-class networking needed? |
| Storage and data | Capacity and throughput, dataset size, upload/download path |
| Location and residency | Country/region constraints, data localization requirements |
| Compliance and access | Sector restrictions, export-control sensitivity, cross-border data rules |
| Timeline | Required start date, acceptable lead time |
| Budget | Budget range or cost target — used for screening, not for squeezing price |

The output is a **requirement specification summary**: what is confirmed, what is assumed, and what you need to confirm internally. Every sourcing and comparison step that follows is measured against it.

## 3. Step two: global sourcing

We do not pre-select a region. Sourcing spans Chinese intelligent-compute centers and GPU clouds as well as serviceable capacity providers in Singapore, Hong Kong, Southeast Asia, the Middle East, Europe and North America, depending on the project.

Sources: our supplier database, public research, industry channels and direct outreach. Every candidate is tagged with its source, retrieval date, and whether it is public information or directly confirmed.

**We do not promise that a resource meeting every condition exists.** If the market cannot deliver, we will tell you so plainly rather than presenting a compromised option as a fit.

## 4. Step three: supplier verification

Before any supplier enters a plan, we verify across five dimensions:

1. **Entity and authorization** — registration, contracting entity, ownership or authorized resale right, export-control declaration.
2. **Resource authenticity** — facility location, deployment evidence, console/monitoring screenshots, independently verifiable information, checkable customer references.
3. **Capacity and models** — model list, sellable quantity, utilization, expansion plans, failure rates.
4. **Delivery and network** — lead time, SLA, bandwidth, interconnect, storage, operations response.
5. **Commercial and compliance** — pricing mechanics, billing unit, payment terms, invoicing, data residency, cross-border clauses.

Results are graded `VERIFIED` / `PARTIALLY VERIFIED` / `REJECTED` / `INSUFFICIENT INFO`, with evidence and open questions recorded per item. **Resources that fail verification do not enter the recommendation.** Where information is missing, we mark it as missing rather than assuming it is fine.

## 5. Step four: comparable plans and risks

Our comparison covers at minimum: location, GPU type and scale, network and storage, lead time, pricing mechanics (not a single headline number), payment terms, supply stability, compliance constraints, principal risks, evidence level, and open items.

Three rules:

- **We do not compare headline unit prices across different billing models** (per-hour, annual, per-cluster and per-token pricing are not directly comparable).
- **We write down what we do not know** rather than filling gaps for the supplier.
- **We make a recommendation and explain it**: which option, why, and what would change our view.

## 6. What we do not promise

To avoid misunderstanding, we explicitly do not promise:

1. **The lowest price.** We provide comparable, verifiable options, not a price guarantee. Market conditions, FX and supply tightness affect final pricing.
2. **A compliance conclusion.** We do not provide legal advice and will not say a plan "is compliant." Export-control, cross-border data and market-access questions must be answered by your legal counsel or a qualified professional firm; we provide factual mapping and a list of open questions.
3. **Availability.** Sellable quantity and delivery dates are subject to the supplier's final written confirmation.
4. **A closing timeline.** Delivery, deployment and network provisioning depend on multiple parties.
5. **Your decision.** We advise; you sign.

## 7. What you get from the first conversation

After a first call of about 30–45 minutes, you receive a **free preliminary resource-matching note** covering:

- which parts of your requirement are clear and which are still missing;
- two or three potential resource directions (region and resource type, not a quotation);
- the main constraints and risks of each direction;
- a recommendation: proceed, gather more information, or defer under current market conditions.

The note is based on public information and initial channel checks. **It is not a formal quotation and not a supply commitment.** Deeper sourcing and verification are paid services; the scope and fee basis are agreed in writing.

## 8. How an engagement runs

First call → requirement specification summary → sourcing and verification → comparison and risk note → commercial coordination (if you decide to proceed) → contracting and delivery follow-up within the agreed scope → optional ongoing procurement management.

Where conditions allow, we recommend that you contract and settle directly with the actual capacity provider. We do not hold your procurement funds.
