# China Compute Globalization Services (CCGS) — Company Introduction

**Working name: China Compute Globalization Services (CCGS)**
**One line: A specialist service provider connecting commercially available Chinese compute capacity with global AI compute demand.**

## 1. Who we are

We are a compute globalization services firm, provisionally named CCGS, to be headquartered in China. We neither trade hardware nor operate data centers. We sit between compute supply and demand, providing demand analysis, capacity sourcing, supplier verification, options comparison and commercial coordination.

We work in two languages and two commercial contexts. We understand how Chinese intelligent-compute centers, GPU clouds and compute operators structure and sell capacity — and we understand how overseas AI companies evaluate technical fit, compliance exposure and procurement risk.

**The company entity is currently being established.** This document is a draft prepared for internal alignment and early-stage communication. It must not be sent to any external party until the CEO has approved it in writing.

## 2. The problem we address

Cross-border compute procurement is rarely about "finding GPUs." Four things must hold at the same time:

1. **The requirement is unclear.** The buyer knows it needs to train a model, but cannot specify card type, count, interconnect, storage or delivery window.
2. **The supply side is opaque.** There are many supplier names, but an outside buyer cannot tell who actually controls capacity and who is another layer of brokerage.
3. **Options are not comparable.** Pricing units, payment terms, network conditions and compliance constraints differ by location; comparing headline unit prices leads to the wrong decision.
4. **The deal does not close.** Language, time zones, contracting conventions and payment paths diverge, so genuine intent stalls.

Our work is to take these four problems apart, verify what can be verified, and translate them into commercial language both sides can act on.

## 3. What we do: two service tracks

### For buyers — compute procurement advisory

Requirement intake → specification definition → global sourcing → supplier verification → options comparison → risk identification → commercial coordination → ongoing procurement management.

What we deliver is not a quotation but a decision-grade view: where each option comes from, what it is based on, what the risks are, and what information is still missing.

### For suppliers — overseas market development

Overseas market research → buyer discovery → qualified demand screening → opportunity introduction → commercial coordination → ongoing channel cooperation.

We help Chinese capacity providers turn "we have capacity" into "we have qualified buyers." Requirements are screened before they reach the supplier, and mismatches are removed early to protect time on both sides.

## 5. What we are not

Clear boundaries build more trust than inflated claims. We do **not**:

- **Own or operate data centers.** We do not run GPU facilities.
- **Hold GPU inventory.** We do not buy and resell hardware.
- **Run a token factory or resell model APIs.** We do not package model access for resale.
- **Hold client procurement funds.** Where conditions allow, we aim for the client and the actual capacity provider to contract and settle directly.
- **Circumvent export controls.** We do not participate in or facilitate any arrangement that evades export controls or trades unauthorized chips.
- **Provide legal advice or claim compliance.** Compliance conclusions must come from qualified professional firms; we provide factual mapping and pathway descriptions.
- **Fabricate.** We do not inflate capacity, client lists, closed deals, partnerships or government backing.

## 6. How we work together

1. **Introductory call (about 30 minutes)** — understand the requirement or the available capacity, and decide whether to continue.
2. **Requirement / resource mapping** — a written summary separating known facts, open questions and key assumptions.
3. **Options and verification** — candidate options or buyer leads within the information available, each marked with its verification status.
4. **Commercial coordination** — once both sides see a fit: introductions, meetings and pre-contract commercial items.
5. **Long-term cooperation** — project-based, success-based introduction or ongoing channel cooperation, agreed case by case in writing.

Fees are success-based. The specific basis is agreed case by case, in writing, reflecting scope, deal size, transaction risk and workload. No pricing is discussed in the first conversation.
