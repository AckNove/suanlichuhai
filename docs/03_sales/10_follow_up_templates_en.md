# Follow-Up Templates (8)

## General rules

- **Cadence:** recap within 24 hours of a first meeting; supplementary material within 72 hours; follow up after 7 days of silence; then every 10–14 days, no more than three times.
- **Principle:** each email moves exactly one thing forward and ends with a single call to action.
- **Record:** log every send in the CRM (date, recipient, content, next step).
- **Never:** quote a specific fee percentage, promise price/capacity/delivery, claim "compliant", "authorised" or "official partner", pressure for signature, or send bulk mail.

---

## Template 1: 24-hour thank-you and recap

**Subject:** Thanks for the conversation — confirming three points ([Company] / [date])

**Body:**
Hi [Name],

Thanks for your time today. To make sure I have it right, here is my recap:

1. Your focus is [workload / resource type], at roughly [volume];
2. The main constraint today is [pain point];
3. You would like [deliverable] from us by [date].

Please correct anything I have misread. My suggested next step is [specific action + timing] — does that work?

Best,
[Name]

**Attachments:** one-page meeting recap with open items; a one-page company introduction only if it came up.
**Avoid:** "You are a valued client"; drawing conclusions on the client's behalf; any pricing in the recap.

---

## Template 2: 72-hour supplementary material

**Subject:** Materials as promised — [document name] ([date])

**Body:**
Hi [Name],

Following our discussion, attached is [document]. The relevant section is on page [X].

- On [question], what we can confirm today is [fact];
- Still outstanding: [item], which I expect to resolve by [date].

If this is useful for your evaluation, I can also prepare [optional item]. Just say the word.

Best,
[Name]

**Attachments:** the document, with version and date noted; a short open-items list.
**Avoid:** "absolutely", "guaranteed", "industry-leading"; third-party data you cannot source.

---

## Template 3: Seven-day no-reply follow-up

**Subject:** Following up — [topic] (a one-line reply is enough)

**Body:**
Hi [Name],

I wanted to check whether [document/recap] reached you. If the timing isn't right, just reply "later" and I'll follow your pace.

If helpful, a 15-minute call on [one specific question] may be the fastest way to decide whether this is worth pursuing.

Best,
[Name]

**Attachments:** none, or re-send the earlier document marked "resend".
**Avoid:** "Why haven't you replied?"; pressure; more than three follow-ups on the same topic.

---

## Template 4: Requirement clarification

**Subject:** Three clarifications on [requirement]

**Body:**
Hi [Name],

To produce options you can compare, I need to confirm three things:

1. [e.g. required start date];
2. [e.g. data residency];
3. [e.g. your deal-breaker].

Once these are set, I can send a first comparison by [date]. If they are not yet decided, that is fine — I will mark them "to be confirmed" and note the impact.

Best,
[Name]

**Attachments:** one-page clarification sheet.
**Avoid:** more than five questions at once; asking for information unrelated to the options; commenting on the client's incumbent supplier.

## Template 5: Sending the options paper

**Subject:** [Client] compute options — A/B/C comparison, [date]

**Body:**
Hi [Name],

Attached is the first version of the comparison. How to read it:

- A/B/C differ in [positioning]; the comparison covers location, GPU model, scale, network, delivery, pricing mechanism and risk;
- Our initial preference is [option] because [two reasons], but the final call depends on verification and your own compliance assessment;
- Page [X] lists five open items; [item] is the one that affects the delivery date.

A 30-minute walkthrough would help, focused on [the main trade-off]. When suits you?

Best,
[Name]

**Attachments:** comparison (PDF plus editable version); risk list; open items.
**Avoid:** "the optimal solution"; any price commitment; hiding unfavourable findings.

## Template 6: Before and after quotation

**6A — Before quoting: agree the basis**

**Subject:** Before the quote — scope and billing basis

**Body:**
Hi [Name],

Before suppliers put numbers on paper, three things to align: (1) billing basis — per GPU, per hour or monthly; (2) whether network, storage and operations are included; (3) quote validity and tax basis.

Once confirmed, I will consolidate the figures into one comparison so we avoid the "cheap headline, expensive total" problem.

**Attachments:** billing-basis confirmation sheet.
**Avoid:** quoting on a supplier's behalf; "roughly how much".

**6B — After quoting: explain the gaps**

**Subject:** The quotes are in — where the differences come from, and what's next

**Body:**
Hi [Name],

All three quotes are consolidated in the attachment. The spread comes mainly from [three reasons, e.g. delivery model, network, commitment term].

Two caveats: these are supplier figures; anything unverified is highlighted in yellow, and [item] still needs written confirmation.

Suggested next step: [verification / clarification / negotiation]. Our own fee is success-based and agreed case by case in writing before any engagement; it is not part of this round's discussion.

**Attachments:** consolidated comparison with source and date; open-items list.
**Avoid:** forwarding only the cheapest number; "we guarantee the lowest price"; omitting ancillary charges.

## Template 7: Pre-signing confirmation

**Subject:** Pre-signing checklist — [client / supplier]

**Body:**
Hi [Name],

Before signing, both sides should confirm the items in the attached checklist:

1. Contracting entity, invoicing and payment path;
2. Delivery scope, timing and acceptance criteria;
3. SLA, remedies and exit terms;
4. Data processing, residency and cross-border clauses;
5. Named contacts and escalation path.

If any item remains open, our recommendation is to hold the signature. We do not hold client funds; the parties should contract and settle directly. Any CCGS service is agreed separately in writing.

**Attachments:** pre-signing checklist; clause-gap summary.
**Avoid:** offering legal advice; "nothing can go wrong"; pushing for signature.

---

## Template 8: Post-deal maintenance

**Subject:** First-week check-in — [project name]

**Body:**
Hi [Name],

One week in, three quick checks: (1) was delivery on plan; (2) is measured performance meeting expectations; (3) is anything stuck?

We will hold a short review on [date] and log each issue with an owner and a date. If you need to expand or adjust later, please just reach out.

**Attachments:** go-live checklist; issue tracker.
**Avoid:** turning the check-in into a sales pitch; promising services outside the agreement; naming the client publicly without consent.

---

## Pre-send checklist

- [ ] Names, company and dates correct
- [ ] One objective, one call to action
- [ ] No fee figures, price promises or compliance conclusions
- [ ] Attachment version and date correct
- [ ] Logged in the CRM, with the next follow-up date set
