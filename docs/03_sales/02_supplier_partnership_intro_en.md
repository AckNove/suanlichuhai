# Supplier Partnership Introduction: Turning Capacity Into Qualified Buyers

## 1. The short version

What we do is straightforward: **we take overseas and cross-border compute demand, screen it from "public lead" into "a requirement worth a conversation," and coordinate it to the point of a commercial discussion.**

We do not promise a number of buyers, we do not promise a closed deal, and we do not ask for your floor price on day one. The first conversation has one purpose: 30 minutes to understand your capacity and decide whether there is a direction worth pursuing.

## 2. What you get

### 2.1 Qualified overseas and cross-border demand

Our target buyers are overseas AI companies with real workloads — training, fine-tuning, inference, rendering, simulation. Demand comes from public market research, industry channels and referrals. Every lead passes the same screening before it reaches you.

### 2.2 Demand screening (the most valuable part)

A requirement must meet all of the following before we introduce it:

- **Technical fit** — card type, scale, interconnect, storage and lead time match what you can actually deliver.
- **Credible scale and budget** — there is a defined budget range or procurement volume, not a casual inquiry.
- **A clear time window** — the buyer knows when it needs capacity and for how long.
- **A decision path** — we can reach a decision-maker, or at least map the decision process.
- **A contracting path** — the buyer is willing to contract and settle directly with the capacity provider where possible.

**If a requirement fails these tests, we remove it before it reaches you.** You will not receive a stream of inquiries that look urgent but have no budget behind them.

### 2.3 Commercial coordination

Language, time zones, contracting conventions, payment paths, technical definitions — we handle that friction: meeting scheduling, materials, clarification, and aligning expectations on both sides up to the pre-contract stage.

### 2.4 Clear boundaries

No exclusivity. No lock on your other channels. No markup between you and the buyer. No holding of funds.

## 3. What we do not need in the first conversation

To respect your time, please do **not** prepare the following for the first call:

- **No commission or pricing discussion.** Fees are not discussed in the first round. If a specific project proceeds to an introduction agreement, the model and fee basis are agreed case by case, in writing.
- **No floor price.** Before there is a concrete requirement, a floor price creates more confusion than value.
- **No customer list.** We will not ask for your existing customer information. If it comes up at all, only to the extent you choose to share.
- **No unverified capacity claims.** "We have plenty of cards" is not useful. We care about verifiable specifics: model, quantity, sellable quantity, deployment status, facility location.
- **No commitment.** This interview is not a contract and creates no exclusivity.

## 4. Cooperation models

| Model | What it means | Best suited to |
|---|---|---|
| Non-exclusive channel | We continuously source and screen overseas / cross-border demand for you within a long-term channel relationship | Suppliers with stable sellable capacity that want a durable overseas entry point |
| Success-based introduction | For a specific project, we introduce and coordinate; fees settle per written agreement after closing | Suppliers with a defined opportunity who prefer case-by-case engagement |
| Project-based | A single client or batch of capacity with agreed scope, timeline and deliverables | Suppliers with a clearly defined, time-bound resource window |

The models can be combined, but scope, responsibilities and settlement must be written down each time. **Neither side is bound until a written agreement is signed.**

## 5. How we judge whether demand is "qualified"

We use a five-stage lead standard internally: public lead → inferred demand → potential opportunity → verified demand → qualified opportunity. Only verified demand or above triggers substantive commercial work. A qualified opportunity requires completed qualification, a reachable decision-maker, a budget/time window and a contracting path.

The same rule applies to us: we do not package an unverified lead as a "client."

## 6. Compliance items we will ask you to confirm

- **Ownership or authorized resale right.** Please state whether you are the capacity owner, an authorized agent, or another role, and provide supporting evidence after the interview where available.
- **Export control self-assessment.** If controlled chips or cross-border delivery are involved, please share your self-assessment status. We do not participate in any arrangement that circumvents export controls.
- **Cross-border data transfer.** If the capacity will process overseas customer data, please describe the current data path and its constraints.
- **Verifiability.** Facility location, deployment status and sellable quantity should be supported by verifiable evidence (photos, console screenshots, third-party confirmation), checked against our verification checklist.

## 7. Next step: a 30-minute capacity interview

**Format:** online call, in Chinese or English.
**Participants:** one or two people who know the capacity and the commercial terms.
**What we will ask about:** entity and authorization, GPU models and sellable quantity, facility and network, delivery lead time, pricing mechanics, payment and invoicing, overseas experience, preferred cooperation model.
**What you get:** verbal feedback on how your capacity reads to a buyer, and — if there is a fit — a description of the demand directions worth pursuing. No fee, no obligation.
**Helpful to have ready:** model list, sellable quantity, facility cities, typical delivery lead time, and whether you currently serve overseas customers (yes/no is enough; no names required).
